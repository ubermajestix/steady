When rejection hits: the reset

Small pushback, criticism, a short reply. Your alarm is loud. Regulate first, think later. You can't reason your way out of a red zone, so don't try yet.

GREENSteady. Thinking clearly.
YELLOWStung. Heating up.
REDFlooded. Want to snap or flee.

First Settle the alarm

  1. Name it (silently): "This is the alarm, not the truth. It will pass."
  2. Breathe, box style: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Repeat until it drops.
  3. Widen the gap: wait 5+ minutes. Don't send, say, or decide anything yet.
  4. Body reset: cold water on the face, a brisk walk, or a longer exhale than inhale.

If you're in the RED, you're not fit to decide, and that's biology, not weakness. Don't answer the email, pick up the phone, or have the conversation. Say only this, then step away:

"I want to give this a real answer. Let me come back to you in a bit."

Then Look again

  • Facts only: what actually happened, no story?
  • What else could it mean? List 2 or 3 other explanations.
  • Just ask: "What did you mean by that?" (Uncertainty is usually the real sting.)
Feeling rejected ≠ being rejected.
Most "rejection" is a conflict of needs, not a verdict on you.
A calm version of you could just say "oh, my bad" and move on. That option is still there once the alarm quiets.
Face to face: pause without turning away. "I can feel myself getting defensive, give me a few minutes, then I'm back to finish this." Then come back. Sorry first (the impact), the "why" later.

Upstream (so it fires less): protect sleep, don't run at full throttle all day, step away from stress before it stacks.
